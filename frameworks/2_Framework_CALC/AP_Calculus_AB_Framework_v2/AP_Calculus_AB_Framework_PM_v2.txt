MAP-FIRST AUTHORING / REPETITION DISCIPLINE
------------------------------------------
For any multi-item / multi-section authoring build, map the full requested scope before final student materials. The map should show exact I-can/evidence jobs, AP topic/condition needs, representation routes, task/scoring structures, and the student reasoning being elicited.

Purposeful repetition is allowed. Repeated derivative, integral, limit, graph-reading, or theorem-condition families may be exactly what students need for fluency or repeated independent evidence. Do not impose anti-repetition quotas and do not rotate graphical/numerical/analytical/verbal forms merely for surface variety.

Use the map to catch template-driven repetition: one AP-looking scaffold, one justification frame, one representation choice, or one form personality repeated across unrelated targets because it is easy to generate. If a repeated structure has a real mapped purpose, keep it. If not, revise the PLAN before authoring. This is a pre-authoring design check, not permission for a late anti-clone rewrite. See `contracts/map_first_repetition_contract.json`.

AP TOPICS SOURCE-COVERAGE TRANSPARENCY
--------------------------------------
The supplied AP Topics corpus is the primary authentic AP-style task/FRQ/scoring reference, but
it does not contain a directly labeled packet for every AP topic grouped into this local 40-section
course map. Direct labeled source gaps are recorded in contracts/framework_qa.json and in each
section reference packet. For those gaps, use the recorded adjacent-topic AP packets only for task
and scoring architecture; use the Assessment Plan + CED + calculus textbook for the actual topic
mathematics. Never claim fallback material is a direct source for the missing topic.

AP AUTHORING RULES
------------------
• AP style is not a decorative label. Tasks should reflect the supplied AP Topic structures:
  representation reading, procedure selection, multipart reasoning, conditions, justification,
  context interpretation, calculator/no-calculator work, and precise communication.
• Theorem/definition conditions are part of the content. Do not use IVT, MVT, EVT, continuity,
  differentiability, or L’Hospital’s Rule as slogans without the required hypotheses/forms.
• Calculator output is not proof. When the supplied CED requires setup for calculator-produced
  FRQ results, the Bank/solution/scoring must preserve that setup expectation.
• Units and interpretation matter in contextual derivative/integral/rate questions.
• Mix graphical, numerical, analytical, and verbal representations when the Assessment Plan
  supports them. A table should be a real table; a graph should be an actual graph.
• Canonical Bank instructional items and Unit Summatives are constructed/open response under the current Universal Bank PM. AP source selected-response examples may inform misconception/task architecture, but do not instantiate them as Bank MC; Blooket is the exception.
• Practice family allocation is map-first. Purposeful repeated families are legitimate when the learning job calls for them. Do not make one form the “justify” form and another the “error-analysis” form, and do not rename one repeated scaffold and call it variety.
• Secure evidence must be semantically independent from exposed instructional content.

LANGUAGE / NOTES
----------------
Use the Physics-textbook tone benchmark but maintain AP Calculus precision. Reading/explanation
should normally use several short paragraphs, not thin one-paragraph exposition or dense walls.
Explain what limits/derivatives/integrals mean and how representations connect before treating
calculus as symbol manipulation when the section calls for conceptual understanding.
