2. EXACT MASTERY SPIRAL — HARD
================================================================

Intro in Unit N -> Review in Unit N+1 -> Mastery in Unit N+2.
Review in Unit N -> Mastery in Unit N+1.

Unit 1 and Unit 2 include readiness/prior-course mastery windows so every Unit can still carry Intro + Review + Mastery simultaneously.

Section numbers do NOT have to match across Units. Place Review/Mastery where they fit the current section best while preserving the exact learning path.

CANONICAL GOAL IDENTITY — HARD
If a goal appears as Review and later Mastery, keep the exact goal ID/title/description. Do not rewrite the goal to make it sound easier/harder. Stage changes support and evidence demand:
  Intro = first learn the idea with support.
  Review = same skill again with less support, a new representation, or a slightly harder context.
  Mastery = independent use; choose representation/method when needed and explain when the task requires it.

Same-day Intro, Review, and Mastery are distinct strands. They do not need to share the same topic.

Future pipeline:
  Unit 7 Intro -> Unit 8 Review -> Next Course Unit 1 Mastery.
  Unit 8 Intro -> Next Course Unit 1 Review -> Next Course Unit 2 Mastery.

Use curriculum/mastery_spiral_mapping.html and curriculum/mastery_goal_map.json as coursewide retrieval aids. Exact Unit Assessment Plan HTML remains primary authority.

3. TWO DIFFERENT SPIRALS
================================================================

Algebra 1 has TWO intentionally different long-term systems:

A. CONCEPTUAL / MASTERY SPIRAL
Owned by the Assessment Plans: Intro -> Review -> Mastery.

B. FLUENCY SPIRAL
Owned primarily by Warm-Ups. It builds durable procedural/foundational capacity across the year and does not need to mirror today's Intro/Review/Mastery strands.

Do not collapse these two systems.

================================================================
4. ALGEBRA WARM-UP ARCHITECTURE — HARD
================================================================

Every normal Algebra Warm-Up uses four stable jobs:

Q1 · SOLVE / EQUATION FLUENCY
A compact solving mini-set. Course progression:
  one-step -> mixed one-step -> two-step -> combining like terms -> distribution -> multi-step -> variables on both sides -> rational coefficients -> cumulative mixed solving.
Older types continue after harder types appear.

Q2 · DIAMOND / FACTORING PATTERN
Number product-sum -> integer factor pairs -> factoring trinomials -> special factoring patterns.
Diamond is a family; blanks/known cells may vary when the mathematics warrants it.

Q3 · SKILL REVIEW
Rotate foundational skills: fractions, integer operations, order of operations, distribution, combining like terms, substitution/evaluation, exponent laws, function notation, and upcoming prerequisites. It may deliberately preteach a useful prerequisite.
For fraction/integer fluency, encourage trying without a calculator first while clearly allowing calculator use if needed.

Q4 · CHALLENGE
Short reasoning/pattern/generalization/error-analysis/representation/nonroutine work. The challenge is not merely ugly arithmetic. Everyone can attempt it; full completion is not required for every student.

Warm-Up routing is NOT 2 Current + 1 Review + 1 Mastery and is NOT an A/B/C stage sampler anymore.

================================================================
5. OFFICIAL EXIT TICKET ARCHITECTURE — HARD
================================================================

A normal Algebra section has ONE official four-question Exit Ticket.

Questions 1–2 = TWO NEW current-Mastery evidence opportunities.
- Select from the current section Mastery Goal(s) and supporting I Can statements.
- Use a coursewide Exit map to distribute I Can coverage intentionally across repeated goal appearances and later evidence routes.
- Do not force every supporting I Can onto one four-question ticket.

Questions 3–4 = TWO FRESH PARALLEL REASSESSMENTS.
- Reassess the two new evidence jobs from the PREVIOUS section's official Exit Ticket.
- Preserve the evidence target and usable difficulty.
- Vary values, representation, context, or structure when useful; do not merely copy exact wording by default.

The chain continues across Unit boundaries:
  Unit N Section 1 normally reassesses Unit N-1 Section 5.

UNIT 1 SECTION 1 OPENING EXCEPTION
There is no previous Algebra Exit Ticket. Do NOT fake two repeats. The exact U1.1 four-question opening pattern is intentionally left unresolved until the upcoming Bank/Exit map discussion. This is a declared open design decision, not permission to reuse the old A/B/C routing.

INTRO / REVIEW FORMATIVE CHECKING
Intro and Review are checked through instruction, Warm-Ups when naturally relevant, Practice, CYU, discussion, whiteboards, activities, teacher observation, and other formative work. If the teacher wants a focused Intro/Review check after reteaching, generate a small targeted RETEACH / RECOVERY CHECK (normally 1–3 focused questions). It is not automatically an official Exit Ticket, Bank requirement, or portfolio event.

Exit Ticket A/B/C as three canonical stage tickets is RETIRED for Algebra 1.

6. EVIDENCE / PORTFOLIO PHILOSOPHY
================================================================

Official Exit Tickets are short rolling mastery evidence, not the only evidence source.
Quick Checks provide later deeper evidence.
Performance Tasks provide richer transfer/modeling evidence where appropriate.
Summatives provide broader secure evidence.
Progress Trackers organize the body of evidence around current Mastery Goals and supporting I Can statements.

Meeting is a professional judgment from sufficient independent evidence, not perfection and not simple averaging. Not Yet / Approaching / Meeting / Exceeding describe the body of evidence, not item-difficulty tiers.

7. BANK STRATEGY — CURRENT DIRECTION
================================================================

The old giant Algebra Bank architecture is retired as a course requirement.

CURRENT PRINCIPLE
Smaller authoritative Bank; stronger map; strongest evidence first.

Author/lock the strongest Exit Ticket and Summative evidence before filling ordinary classroom inventory. Practice and instruction then prepare students for those mapped evidence demands.

Current roles:
- Practice 1 = primary independent practice aligned to the section evidence/learning plan.
- Extra Practice = recovery and/or extension.
- CYU remains useful.
- WTC remains a useful recurring plan/route.
- Practice 2 and Practice 3 are no longer required canonical Bank files.
- Middle practice (stations, Find Someone Who, matching, sorting, task cards, partner sets, whiteboards, etc.) may be generated at artifact build time.
- Notes do not require six premade canonical Example/YTI pairs. Examples/YTIs may be purpose-authored/refined during the Notes build from the Assessment Plan, representations, misconceptions, Question Structure, and useful canonical source material.

IMPORTANT: exact new Bank counts/package mechanics are intentionally deferred to the NEXT Bank Map / Bank PM revision. Do not revive legacy 139-per-section or 24/24/24/24 counts while that revision is pending.

8. REPRESENTATION SYSTEM
================================================================

Graph · Equation · Table · Context are the core Algebra forms of meaning.
Use the applicable forms substantively and translate among them when the exact I Can supports it. Representation use is not an equal-percentage quota.

Function language develops across the course:
  input/output -> function/not a function -> f(x) -> evaluation -> domain/range -> key features.

Domain/range is woven where natural rather than isolated as a standalone section.
Exponent laws are explicit in polynomial/exponential work and supported by Warm-Up Skill Review.
Radicals/irrational values stay lean and appear naturally in quadratic solving.
Statistics stays light and model-focused.
Literal-equation rearranging and sequences do not require standalone sections under the current course map.

10. QUESTION / LANGUAGE / VALUES
================================================================

Canonical instructional and Summative Algebra items are constructed/open response by default. Blooket or another explicitly authorized game export may use selected response.

Use natural high-school language and simple concrete verbs. Prefer input/output and ordered-pair language where useful. Use f(x) naturally without making wording artificially formal.

Choose pedagogically natural values. Difficulty should come from the Algebra, not arbitrary random inflation.

Use the current universal Question Structure for reasoning, comparison, misconception diagnosis, modeling, transfer, representation connections, and coherent multipart work. Direct questions should stay direct when that is the right evidence job.
