Review Station 1 - Research Before You Build - Answer Key

Question 1

A marble moves too fast on the first drop. Name one design factor you would research and predict how changing it could affect speed.

Answer: Example: reduce the slope angle; a shallower slope can reduce acceleration/speed. Other plausible causal factors are valid.

Question 2

Classify: 'The base must fit in 12 in x 12 in.' Is this a requirement or a research factor?

Answer: Requirement/constraint.

Question 3

Classify: 'Tighter turns may make the marble leave the track.' Is this a requirement or a research factor?

Answer: Research/design factor because it predicts how a variable affects performance.

Question 4

For a long-span structure, explain one reason diagonal bracing might matter before you build.

Answer: It can reduce deformation/racking and help redirect/spread forces through the structure.

Review Station 2 - Brainstorm Real Alternatives - Answer Key

Question 1

Idea A is a zig-zag ramp. Give a genuinely different Idea B for slowing a marble.

Answer: Example: spiral/funnel path, bowl-and-ramp path, or another substantially different geometry.

Question 2

Why is changing only the color, tape placement, or small decoration not enough to count as a second engineering brainstorm?

Answer: Those changes do not create a meaningfully different solution strategy or performance mechanism.

Question 3

Give one advantage and one risk of a long shallow path.

Answer: Advantage: may increase travel time. Risk: may stop if too shallow or may require more space/material.

Question 4

Give one advantage and one risk of a steep compact path.

Answer: Advantage: easier to keep moving / compact. Risk: may be too fast or harder to keep on the path.

Review Station 3 - Make It Better From Evidence - Answer Key

Question 1

The marble leaves the outside of the same turn in 2 of 3 trials. Propose one specific redesign.

Answer: Example: add/tall-en the outside wall or increase the turn radius at that turn.

Question 2

The marble sticks at taped seams in repeated trials. Propose one specific redesign.

Answer: Smooth/reduce the seams, change how sections join, or move tape away from the rolling surface.

Question 3

A bridge holds but bends at midspan while joints stay firm. What should the redesign target?

Answer: The midspan/load path, such as adding bracing or strengthening the central members, not the already-secure joints.

Question 4

Why is 'make it stronger' weaker than 'add a diagonal brace across the bending side panel'?

Answer: The second is specific, buildable, and targets an observed failure mechanism.

Review Station 4 - Write the Evidence Chain - Answer Key

Question 1

Complete: Because the test showed the marble fell off the final turn twice, I will ___, which should ___.

Answer: Example: add a taller outside wall; keep the marble on the path through the turn.

Question 2

Complete: Because the structure bent in the center, I will ___, which should ___.

Answer: Example: add diagonal/central bracing; reduce bending and spread the load.

Question 3

What three parts should a strong Make It Better explanation contain?

Answer: Specific test observation/evidence, specific design change, and predicted effect/reason.

Question 4

Rewrite 'It did not work, so I changed it' as a stronger evidence-based statement.

Answer: Any response that names what failed, the exact change, and why that change should address the failure.

Extension Station 5 - Transfer Across Challenges - Answer Key

Question 1

A marble track and a long-span bridge are different challenges. What part of the engineering process should stay the same after the first test?

Answer: Use observed evidence to identify a specific weakness, make a targeted redesign, and retest the predicted effect.

Question 2

A marble track is reliable but too fast. A bridge is strong but bends too much. For each, write the performance variable you would target next.

Answer: Marble: travel time/speed while preserving reliability. Bridge: stiffness/deformation or load path while preserving span/strength.

Question 3

Why can a successful first test still justify a redesign?

Answer: A design can meet the minimum requirement but still be optimized for the success measure, efficiency, reliability, or margin.

Question 4

Give one example of a redesign that improves one criterion but could hurt another. Name the tradeoff.

Answer: Example: making a marble slope steeper improves reliable motion but can reduce travel time; adding material can strengthen a bridge but increase mass/material use.

Extension Station 6 - Optimize With Data - Answer Key

Question 1

Version 1 best time = 10 s; Version 2 best time = 13 s. Find the improvement in seconds.

Answer: 3 s.

Question 2

For the same data (10 s to 13 s), find the percent increase relative to Version 1.

Answer: (13-10)/10 = 0.30 = 30%.

Question 3

Version 1 succeeds 3/3 trials at 10 s. Version 2 succeeds 1/3 trials at 14 s. Is Version 2 clearly better? Explain.

Answer: No. It has a longer successful time but much worse reliability; the evidence shows a tradeoff, not a clear overall improvement.

Question 4

A redesign improves reliability but reduces travel time slightly. What additional test or change would you plan next?

Answer: Retest multiple trials while adjusting slope/path length/friction to recover time without losing the reliability gain; a specific evidence-based plan is valid.
