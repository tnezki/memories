Set 1 - Question 1
Which is stronger for an engineering decision: an unsupported opinion or a test result collected under controlled conditions? Explain.
Teacher support
Answer: The controlled test result, because it provides observable evidence tied to the design.
Teacher move: Ask what makes the evidence usable for a decision.
Student discourse move: Compare reliability, relevance, and specificity.

Set 1 - Question 2
A slow-marble device must keep the marble moving for at least 20 seconds. Is 20 seconds a criterion or a constraint?
Teacher support
Answer: Criterion.
Teacher move: Ask whether the statement defines success or limits resources/conditions.
Student discourse move: Explain the difference between success criteria and constraints.

Set 1 - Question 3
Why should a team generate several ideas before choosing one?
Teacher support
Answer: It creates alternatives that can be compared against criteria, constraints, and evidence.
Teacher move: Ask students what is lost when the first idea is accepted automatically.
Student discourse move: Compare quantity of ideas with quality of final selection.

Set 1 - Question 4
Design A is easy to build but inconsistent. Design B is harder to build but repeatedly meets the criterion. Which evidence matters most for choosing?
Teacher support
Answer: Repeated performance against the criterion, while still considering feasibility/constraints.
Teacher move: Ask which information is tied most directly to the goal.
Student discourse move: Defend a choice using at least two criteria.

Set 1 - Question 5
A prototype succeeds once and fails three times. What should the team conclude?
Teacher support
Answer: The design is not yet reliable; investigate the failure pattern and repeat controlled tests.
Teacher move: Ask whether one success outweighs repeated failures.
Student discourse move: Discuss reliability versus one-time performance.

Set 1 - Question 6
Why is changing one major design variable at a time useful during redesign?
Teacher support
Answer: It makes cause-and-effect easier to interpret.
Teacher move: Ask what becomes unclear when several changes happen at once.
Student discourse move: Use a controlled-test example.

Set 1 - Question 7
A source says friction increases when surfaces are rougher. How could that information guide a slow-marble redesign?
Teacher support
Answer: A team could test a rougher contact surface to increase friction and slow the marble, while controlling other variables.
Teacher move: Require a specific testable change.
Student discourse move: Connect source evidence to a measurable design test.

Set 1 - Question 8
A redesign improves average run time from 15 s to 24 s across three trials. Write a brief claim-evidence-reasoning defense.
Teacher support
Answer: Claim: redesign improved performance. Evidence: average rose 9 s to 24 s. Reasoning: repeated controlled trials indicate the change likely improved the target behavior.
Teacher move: Ask for all three CER parts.
Student discourse move: Compare a data-only answer with a reasoned design defense.
