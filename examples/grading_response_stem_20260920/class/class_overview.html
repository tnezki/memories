Engineering Design Process - Research, Brainstorming, and Redesign

10 student evidence sets across two engineering mini-challenges

Evidence Analyzed

The submitted record sheets were reviewed with emphasis on Research / What Should We Think About?, Brainstorming, and Make It Better. No rubric was supplied, so scores are teacher-review recommendations based on completeness, specificity, causal reasoning, and evidence use in those sections.

Major Strengths

  • Most students generated at least two visual solution ideas before or during prototyping.
  • Several students identified relevant physical/design variables such as slope, friction, turns, walls, supports, and path length.
  • The strongest packets used a concrete test failure to drive a targeted redesign rather than simply rebuilding the same design.

Top Actionable Patterns

  • Research notes often list nouns or constraints instead of cause-and-effect predictions about performance.
  • Some brainstorms are variations of the same idea or leave the second alternative incomplete, limiting meaningful comparison.
  • Make It Better is the clearest class-wide leverage point: students need to cite the exact test observation, name the specific change, and predict its effect.
  • Several packets record redesign ideas without enough Version 1 evidence to show why that change was selected.
Highest-leverage class target: Use the evidence chain: 'Because the test showed ___, I will change ___, which should ___.'
