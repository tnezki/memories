Natalie - Individual PracticeName: ____________________

Extend your strong design process by making the evidence-to-reasoning link more precise.

1. Cause and effect A tower with a wide base stands for 10 seconds, while a similar tower with a narrow base tips. Explain one scientific reason the wider base can be more stable.

2. Use test data Trial A is 88 cm and stands for 10 seconds. Trial B is 64 cm and falls before 10 seconds. Write one evidence sentence that supports the claim that Trial A was the more successful design.

3. CER reasoning Complete the reasoning: 'A wider base can make a tower more stable because ...' Your explanation should describe how the design resists tipping.

4. Design extension You must still use only 8 straws and 5 paper clips. Describe one change that could increase height without giving up the wide, stable base. Explain the trade-off you would watch for during testing.

Leah - Individual PracticeName: ____________________

Focus on turning observations and data into a clear claim-evidence-reasoning explanation.

1. Evidence from data Trial 1 is 28 cm and does not stand for 10 seconds. Trial 2 is 30 cm and does stand for 10 seconds. Which trial better meets the challenge, and what exact data supports your choice?

2. Reasoning Explain how a low center of gravity can help a tower resist tipping. Use the words 'center of mass' or 'weight' and 'base.'

3. Fix a CER A student writes: Claim: 'My design worked.' Evidence: 'It was sturdy.' Rewrite the evidence so it uses a measurable test result.

4. Fair redesign test You want to test whether a wider base improves stability. Describe what you would change and name two things you would keep the same so the comparison is fair.

Charlie - Individual PracticeName: ____________________

Keep the strong testing process while tightening constraint-checking and scientific reasoning.

1. Criteria and constraints The goal is the tallest free-standing tower using only 8 straws and 5 paper clips. Identify the criterion and both material/stability constraints in this statement.

2. Constraint check A redesign uses 8 straws and 6 paper clips. Explain why it cannot be accepted as the final solution, even if it is the tallest tower.

3. Interpret trials A tower stands at 77 cm and 85 cm in two trials but falls at 83.5 cm in another. Give one reasonable conclusion about what the data says about height and stability.

4. Improve the reasoning Write a reasoning sentence that explains how strong joints between straws can improve tower stability while staying within the 5-paper-clip limit.

Dylan - Individual PracticeName: ____________________

Use your good structural ideas to write more precise evidence and reasoning.

1. Use numerical evidence Version 1 stands at 51 cm. Version 2 stands at 89 cm. Write one evidence sentence showing why Version 2 better met the height criterion while still meeting the 10-second stability requirement.

2. Triangular bracing Explain why adding triangular bracing near the base can make a tower more rigid than an unbraced four-sided frame.

3. CER reasoning Complete this reasoning: 'The reinforced bottom helped the tower stand because ...' Describe what happened to bending, wobbling, or tipping.

4. Constraint-aware redesign Describe one way to rearrange the same 8 straws and 5 paper clips to gain height while keeping a stable base. Explain what you would test next.
