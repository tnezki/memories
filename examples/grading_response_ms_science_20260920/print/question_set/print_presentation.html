Set 1 - Question 1
The challenge is to build the tallest free-standing tower using only 8 straws and 5 paper clips, and the tower must stand on its own for 10 seconds. Identify the criterion and the constraints.
Teacher support
Answer: Criterion: maximize tower height. Constraints: use only 8 straws and 5 paper clips, the tower must be free-standing, and it must remain standing for 10 seconds.
Teacher move: Ask students to underline the desired outcome once and box every limit or rule.
Student discourse move: Compare your criterion/constraint list with a partner and justify any difference.

Set 1 - Question 2
Two towers have the same height and use the same materials. Tower A has a wide base; Tower B has a very narrow base. Which design is generally less likely to tip, and why?
Teacher support
Answer: Tower A. A wider support base gives the center of mass more room to remain above the support area before the tower tips, so small leaning or wobbling is less likely to move it past the edge of the base.
Teacher move: Press for a mechanism; do not accept only 'because it is wider.'
Student discourse move: Use the frame: 'A wider base matters because when the tower leans...'.

Set 1 - Question 3
Three designs are tested: A = 82 cm and stands 10 seconds; B = 91 cm and falls after 6 seconds; C = 88 cm and stands 10 seconds. Which design best meets the challenge? Explain with data.
Teacher support
Answer: Design C. It is the tallest design that also satisfies the 10-second standing constraint: 88 cm and 10 seconds.
Teacher move: Ask which results are disqualified before comparing heights.
Student discourse move: State the winning design, then cite both the height and the 10-second result.

Set 1 - Question 4
A team wants to test whether base width affects stability. Describe a fair comparison: what should they change, and name at least two important things they should keep the same.
Teacher support
Answer: Change the base width. Keep other important variables the same, such as the number/type of straws and clips, tower height or overall design above the base, connection method, testing surface, and the 10-second test procedure.
Teacher move: Separate the independent variable from controlled variables on the board.
Student discourse move: Trade answers and circle the one variable that is intentionally changed.

Set 1 - Question 5
A tower stands at 76 cm for 10 seconds. After a wider-base redesign, it stands at 84 cm for 10 seconds. Write one strong evidence sentence for the claim that the redesign improved performance.
Teacher support
Answer: After the redesign, the tower increased from 76 cm to 84 cm and still met the 10-second standing requirement.
Teacher move: Require numbers and the stability result; avoid accepting 'it was better.'
Student discourse move: Read your evidence to a partner; the partner checks for a measurement and the success condition.

Set 1 - Question 6
Why can adding a diagonal brace to make triangles reduce wobbling in a tower frame?
Teacher support
Answer: A triangle keeps its shape unless a side changes length, while an unbraced rectangle can shear into a slanted shape. Diagonal bracing therefore makes the frame more rigid and reduces wobbling.
Teacher move: Have students demonstrate with finger-made rectangle and triangle shapes before explaining.
Student discourse move: Explain the difference between 'stronger material' and 'stronger shape.'

Set 1 - Question 7
A team's 90 cm tower falls after 7 seconds, while its 82 cm tower stands for 10 seconds. They want a taller valid design using the same materials. Propose one redesign and explain what trade-off they should monitor.
Teacher support
Answer: One valid redesign is to keep or widen/reinforce the lower base while rearranging upper straws to gain height. The team must monitor the trade-off that extra height raises the center of mass and can increase tipping or wobble, so the new design must still stand for 10 seconds.
Teacher move: Require the proposal to respect the same material limits and name a measurable next test.
Student discourse move: Challenge a partner's redesign: 'How does yours gain height without losing the 10-second constraint?'

Set 1 - Question 8
A team proposes using a ninth straw because it makes the tower 10 cm taller and still stable. Can that be the final solution? If not, revise the idea so it follows the challenge rules.
Teacher support
Answer: No. A ninth straw violates the 8-straw constraint. The team must remove the extra straw and rearrange the original 8 straws—for example, preserving a braced base while using the remaining straws more vertically—and then retest height and 10-second stability.
Teacher move: Ask students to distinguish 'performs well' from 'is a valid solution.'
Student discourse move: Use the sentence: 'The idea fails the constraint because..., so I would revise it by...'.
