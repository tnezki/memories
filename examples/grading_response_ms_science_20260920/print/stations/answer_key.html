Review Station 1 - Criteria and Constraints - Answer Key

Question 1

A bridge challenge says: span 30 cm, hold 500 g, and use no more than 20 craft sticks. Name one criterion and two constraints.

Answer: Criterion: successfully span/hold the required load (or maximize performance if stated). Constraints include the 30 cm span requirement and no more than 20 craft sticks; holding 500 g can also be treated as a required performance constraint.

Question 2

For the tower challenge, a design uses 8 straws, 5 paper clips, reaches 95 cm, but must be held by a hand. Is it valid? Why?

Answer: No. It violates the free-standing constraint even though the height is strong.

Question 3

A tower uses 7 straws and 5 paper clips and stands 10 seconds. Does using fewer than 8 straws violate the material rule 'use only 8 straws and 5 paper clips'?

Answer: No, if 'only' means no more than the supplied amounts. It stays within the limit; the teacher can clarify if the classroom rule required using all pieces.

Question 4

Write one question a team should ask before building so it does not accidentally violate a constraint.

Answer: Example: 'Does our plan use no more than 8 straws and 5 paper clips and stand without outside support for 10 seconds?'

Review Station 2 - Read the Test Data - Answer Key

Question 1

Tower A: 70 cm, yes for 10 s. Tower B: 83 cm, no. Which currently meets the challenge better?

Answer: Tower A, because it satisfies the stability requirement; B is disqualified by falling.

Question 2

Tower C: 74 cm, yes. Tower D: 81 cm, yes. Which is the better result under the tallest-valid-tower criterion?

Answer: Tower D at 81 cm because both are valid and 81 cm is taller.

Question 3

A tower improves from 62 cm stable to 78 cm stable. By how many centimeters did the successful height increase?

Answer: 16 cm.

Question 4

Why should a data table record both height and whether the tower stood for 10 seconds?

Answer: Height measures the criterion, while the stand-time result checks the required stability constraint; both are needed to judge success.

Review Station 3 - Stability Science - Answer Key

Question 1

Which is generally more stable: a wide-base tower or a narrow-base tower of the same height? Explain.

Answer: The wide-base tower, because the center of mass can move farther sideways before passing beyond the support base and causing a tip.

Question 2

How can lowering heavy parts of a tower affect stability?

Answer: It lowers the center of mass, so a larger tilt is usually needed before the center of mass moves beyond the base.

Question 3

Why does a diagonal brace help an unbraced rectangular frame?

Answer: It forms triangles and resists shearing, making the frame more rigid and reducing wobble.

Question 4

A very tall tower has a narrow base. Name two design changes that could improve stability without adding materials.

Answer: Examples: widen the base by rearranging lower straws, add/reposition diagonal bracing, lower more material toward the bottom, or shorten/reconfigure the upper section.

Review Station 4 - Build a Better CER - Answer Key

Question 1

Choose the stronger evidence: (A) 'It was sturdy.' (B) 'It stood for 10 seconds at 79 cm.' Explain.

Answer: B, because it gives measurable evidence tied directly to the success criteria/constraints.

Question 2

Claim: 'The redesign improved the tower.' Data: before = 68 cm stable, after = 80 cm stable. Write the evidence sentence.

Answer: The stable tower height increased from 68 cm to 80 cm after the redesign.

Question 3

Write a reasoning sentence connecting a wider base to less tipping.

Answer: Example: A wider base lets the center of mass shift farther before it moves beyond the support area, so the tower is less likely to tip.

Question 4

What is missing from this CER? Claim: 'Triangles helped.' Evidence: 'The second tower stood 10 seconds at 86 cm.'

Answer: Reasoning that explains why triangular bracing would cause greater rigidity/stability and reduce wobble or tipping.

Extension Station 5 - Optimize the Design - Answer Key

Question 1

Design A is 84 cm stable. Design B is 92 cm but falls after 8 seconds. What should the team optimize next, and why?

Answer: Try to gain height while restoring at least 10-second stability; B shows height improved but the stability constraint is not yet met.

Question 2

Propose one change that could make B more stable without adding materials.

Answer: Examples: widen/reinforce the base, move bracing lower, add diagonal bracing by rearranging existing straws, or reduce top-heavy structure.

Question 3

What measurement should the team collect after the redesign?

Answer: At minimum, tower height and whether/how long it stands, using the same 10-second test.

Question 4

Why is 'make it much shorter' not automatically the best redesign even if it becomes stable?

Answer: The challenge also has a height criterion; a good solution balances stability with maximizing valid height.

Extension Station 6 - Engineering Decision - Answer Key

Question 1

A team has two valid towers: 87 cm with noticeable wobble and 85 cm with very little wobble. Which should they submit under a rule that only requires standing 10 seconds and rewards greatest height?

Answer: 87 cm, provided it actually completes the 10-second free-standing test, because it is the taller valid tower under the stated rules.

Question 2

What additional classroom rule could make the 85 cm design preferable?

Answer: A rule about extra stability margin, resisting a disturbance, supporting a load, or remaining standing longer could favor the less-wobbly design.

Question 3

A student wants to add tape even though tape is not listed as an allowed material. What should the team do?

Answer: Do not use it unless the teacher explicitly changes the constraints; redesign using the allowed materials.

Question 4

Write one final design recommendation that includes a feature, evidence to collect, and the constraint check.

Answer: Example: 'Rearrange two lower straws into diagonal braces, then measure height and confirm the tower stands for 10 seconds while still using no more than 8 straws and 5 paper clips.'
