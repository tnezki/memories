Teacher Guide - Common Worksheet

Tallest Tower Engineering Challenge

Review + Extension / Transfer

1. Review - Criteria and constraints

A tower must be at least 40 cm tall and may use no more than 25 straws. Identify the criterion and the constraint.
Answer: Criterion: at least 40 cm tall. Constraint: no more than 25 straws.
Teacher move: Ask students to underline what defines success and circle what limits the design.
Student discourse move: Compare how criteria and constraints play different roles in a design challenge.

2. Review - Fair testing

Two tower designs are being compared. Name two conditions that should stay the same so the test is fair.
Answer: Examples: same materials, same table, same test time, same disturbance method.
Teacher move: Press for variables that directly affect the comparison.
Student discourse move: Explain why changing several variables at once weakens the conclusion.

3. Review - Evidence

Tower A stands 9 s and Tower B stands 27 s. Which design has stronger evidence of stability?
Answer: Tower B, because it remained standing 18 s longer.
Teacher move: Require the number in the explanation.
Student discourse move: Compare a vague claim with an evidence-based claim.

4. Review - Failure analysis

A tower repeatedly bends at the same joint before falling. What should the team investigate first?
Answer: The repeated weak joint and how it is connected/reinforced.
Teacher move: Ask what the repeated pattern tells us about cause.
Student discourse move: Defend why the failure point is more useful than guessing a random redesign.

5. Review - Cause and effect

Explain one reason a wider base can make a tall tower more stable.
Answer: A wider base increases support and makes tipping less likely by keeping the center of mass over the base for a larger range of tilt.
Teacher move: Ask students to connect geometry to tipping.
Student discourse move: Use “because” to link the change to the effect.

6. Review - Redesign choice

A tower is stable but too short. Which redesign best targets the problem: widen the base, increase height while preserving bracing, or add weight at the bottom?
Answer: Increase height while preserving bracing, because the unmet criterion is height.
Teacher move: Ask which criterion is not yet met.
Student discourse move: Explain why the other changes do not directly solve the stated problem.

7. Extension - Tradeoffs

A redesign is taller but less stable. Explain the tradeoff and propose one next test.
Answer: The design improved height but reduced stability; next, test a bracing change while keeping height constant.
Teacher move: Ask students to name both the gain and the cost.
Student discourse move: Compare redesigns that change one variable at a time.

8. Extension - Evidence-based redesign

A tower lasts 10 s, then 18 s after adding cross-bracing. What should the team conclude and test next?
Answer: Cross-bracing likely improved stability; repeat the test or vary bracing while holding other conditions constant.
Teacher move: Ask what the data supports versus what it proves.
Student discourse move: Discuss why repeated trials strengthen the conclusion.
