Tallest Tower Engineering Challenge

Evidence Analyzed

Four three-page Tallest Tower Engineering Challenge packets were reviewed. Students were evaluated on evidence of the engineering design process, use of criteria/constraints, testing, iteration, and claim-evidence-reasoning from the submitted work.

Major Strengths

  • All four students identified at least one plausible stability feature such as a wide base, lower center of gravity, stronger bottom support, or triangular structure.
  • All four recorded test results and distinguished whether a tower met the 10-second free-standing requirement.
  • Students generally used testing to suggest a next design change rather than treating the first build as final.

Top Actionable Patterns

  • CER evidence is often qualitative when numerical height and stand-time data are available.
  • Reasoning frequently names a feature ('wide base,' 'sturdy,' 'stable bottom') without explaining the cause-and-effect mechanism that reduces tipping.
  • Redesign ideas should be checked explicitly against the fixed material constraints before being accepted.
Highest-leverage class target: Move from 'what feature helped' to 'what the data shows and why that feature caused greater stability.'
