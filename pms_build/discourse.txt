======================================================================
3. PURPOSE — DISCOURSE IS EMBEDDED FACILITATION
======================================================================

Discourse support helps the teacher make student thinking visible and productive around worthwhile existing work.

Good discourse may help students:
- notice;
- predict;
- compare;
- justify;
- critique;
- connect representations;
- interpret evidence;
- revise a claim/model;
- synthesize what the class learned;
- surface a misconception productively.

Discourse is NOT:
- extra filler questions;
- a generic script pasted into every teacher guide;
- a second curriculum sequence;
- a separate student assignment;
- an excuse to turn independent evidence into coached evidence.

The teacher remains free to skip, shorten, adapt, or ignore any discourse move.

5. INDEPENDENT-EVIDENCE EXCLUSION — HARD
======================================================================

Do not add facilitation that contaminates evidence intended to be independent.

- Portfolio scoring/judgment workflow;

  FAIL CLOSED on that proposed discourse insertion rather than weaken evidence validity.

======================================================================
7. POINT-OF-USE RULE — HARD
======================================================================

Discourse belongs next to the exact teacher-guide phase it supports.

Do NOT create one detached Unit-wide appendix of generic discourse prompts when the teacher needs the support during a specific task.

Use the EXISTING teacher-guide structure and current CSS hooks.
Do not invent a universal Discourse CSS system.
Do not add a new shared stylesheet.
Do not restyle the guide.

Possible teacher-facing components include only what is useful for that moment:

TEACHER MOVES
- concise facilitation actions;
- normally 2–4 when needed;
- protect productive struggle;
- sequence noticing/pressing/connecting intentionally.

STUDENT MOVES
- likely productive student actions or talk;
- normally 2–4 when needed;
- describe useful participation, not a scripted performance.

LISTEN FOR
- strong reasoning/evidence;
- important misconception;
- representation connection;
- language indicating partial understanding.

PRESS / FOLLOW-UP
- content-specific questions that deepen or clarify thinking;
- do not ask a generic “why?” after every prompt;
- do not reveal the answer through the question.

ACCEPTABLE RESPONSES / SYNTHESIS
- use only when the teacher needs calibration;
- distinguish one strong example from criteria for other acceptable responses;
- do not imply only one exact student sentence counts.

Do NOT force all headings/components into every moment.
A short, well-placed Teacher Move may be better than a full discourse block.

======================================================================
8. CONTENT QUALITY — HARD
======================================================================

Every discourse insertion must be specific to the actual student content in front of the teacher.

BAD:
- “Have students discuss with a partner.”
- “Ask students to explain their reasoning.”
- “Circulate and monitor.”
repeated with no content-specific purpose.

GOOD discourse identifies what students are comparing/noticing/justifying and what the teacher should listen for or press next.

Discourse should:
- use the exact current course language;
- respect the exact I-can / learning job;
- use current representations as reasoning objects when appropriate;
- surface misconceptions without teaching the misconception as fact;
- help the teacher decide what to press, connect, or leave unresolved;
- preserve student intellectual ownership.

Do not turn a concise teacher guide into a dense script.
Useful brevity is preferred.
