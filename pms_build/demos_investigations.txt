3. RESOURCE PURPOSE — PHILOSOPHY LOCK
======================================================================

DEMO
- optional 5–8 minute projector/screen-first curiosity or intellectual-tension hook unless Framework says otherwise;
- students notice, predict, compare, argue, observe, confront intuition, or ask a useful question;
- does NOT need to teach the whole section;
- ends with a short discussion/carry-forward moment, normally 2–4 worthwhile questions or prompts;
- may use a physical object, phenomenon, image, graph, table, model, equation, data display, or teacher reveal;
- must not become a mini-lecture or quiz.

INVESTIGATION
- optional student learning experience, not renamed Practice;
- students DO something substantive: collect/organize data, test a claim, compare models, build/translate representations,
  sort/classify, investigate a pattern, use a simulation/tool, make/revise a prediction, or conduct a short physical experiment;
- the learning flow is custom to the section;
- no universal task count or forced Part 1/2/3 choreography;
- synthesis/debrief should articulate what emerged when useful;
- must not become the only place required core instruction or secure evidence exists.

CUSTOM ARTIFACT RULE:
Demos and Investigations may be purpose-built because they are inherently custom instructional resources.
Stable course CSS controls visual language; intellectual choreography remains section-specific.

INVESTIGATION PLAN

- concrete student artifact/output;
- representations read/built/used/compared;
- graph/figure/data/tool decision;
- materials/technology;
- 3–6 step experience sequence in plain language;
- one-sentence reason the experience fits the exact section learning job.

GRAPH / FIGURE / DATA DECISION — choose one:
- TOOL-GENERATED STUDENT EVIDENCE;
- TOOL-GENERATED TEACHER REVEAL;
- STUDENT-CONSTRUCTED REPRESENTATION;
- NO GRAPH/FIGURE NEEDED, with brief reason.

WHOLE-SCOPE PLAN QA:
- no cloned investigation choreography caused by template convenience;
- student action matches exact section learning job;
- representation-heavy learning is not accidentally text-only;
- named actions are real: COLLECT means collect, BUILD means build, MOVE means movement, COMPARE means actual comparison;
- repetition is allowed when instructionally purposeful;
- no novelty quota.

Repair the PLAN before rendering.

Plan artifacts are BUILD WORKING FILES, not normal course-repository outputs.
Do not add plan JSON/HTML to the course root unless the teacher explicitly requests them.

5. SOURCE / REPRESENTATION RULES
======================================================================

Use only current Framework-declared source/reference material.
Do not perform outside research.

Source hierarchy comes from the Physics Framework.
Do not let source material expand the Unit scope.

Representations are instructional content.
Students must actually read/build/use/compare/annotate/interpret them.

If the Framework declares an authoritative graph/figure/data tool:

- do not search for alternate tools;

- do not label, mark, draw, rank, calculate, or highlight the feature students must determine;

NO DESCRIPTIVE STUDENT CAPTIONS:

Do not put explanatory/source captions beneath student-facing figures.

9. INVESTIGATION TEACHER GUIDE
======================================================================

Use ONLY:
  ../../css/teacher_guide.css

Same `.guide` shell as Demo teacher guides.

Start with practical overview:
- Why This Fits;
- time/grouping;
- materials;
- setup;
- suggested flow;
- expected evidence/observations;
- common problems/misconceptions;
- strong synthesis/debrief target.

Then walk through the substantive student phases in order.
For each phase where teacher reference is useful:
- identify the student task/representation;
- give strong expected/acceptable response or observation;
- explain why / what to listen for;
- include teacher press questions when useful.

For open-ended work:
- give one strong sample;
- give criteria for other acceptable responses.

For multi-representation work:
- explain how the representations should agree.

For discussion-worthy phases include:
- Teacher Moves;
- Student Moves;
- Listen For when useful.

Student files never contain teacher discourse blocks.
