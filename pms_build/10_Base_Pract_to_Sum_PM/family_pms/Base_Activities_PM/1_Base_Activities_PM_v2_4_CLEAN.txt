APPROVED PARTICIPATION-STRUCTURE LIBRARY
================================================================

Use the following as the approved universal library. Preserve the structure/setup/directions/goal wording unless the current Framework explicitly supplies a newer course-specific version. Do NOT invent names such as "Representation Relay" or other unapproved activities in a normal build.

PROJECTION / WHITEBOARD OPTIONS

◆ Whiteboard Indy
Structure: Individual -> Independent practice
Setup: Each student gets a whiteboard and one marker.
Directions:
- Notes are welcome.
- Try something.
- Write large and clearly - your work should be readable from across the room.
- Use your resources: ask your partner first, then the teacher. Do not copy.
- Mistakes are part of learning - fix them together and discuss what went wrong.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry

◆ Rally Coach
Structure: Partner explanation + alternating roles
Setup: Sit with your partner. Each person needs one whiteboard and one marker.
Directions:
- Work with a partner.
- Partner A explains, Partner B writes.
- Switch roles.
- Check answers with teacher.
- Rotate partners.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy

◆ Whiteboard Partners
Structure: Fast partner practice
Setup: Work with your partner. One whiteboard and one marker between you.
Directions:
- Work with a partner.
- Both students stay engaged.
- Alternate who writes.
- Write clearly and large.
- Stay focused and collaborate.
- Fix mistakes and discuss.
Goal: Engagement + quick feedback

◆ Speed Dating Math
Structure: Paired practice -> Timed rotation -> Strategy sharing
Setup: Partners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person.
Directions:
- Work with a partner on the projected problem - both students write on their own paper.
- Compare strategies and methods with your partner before rotating.
- After 3-4 minutes, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing.
- Bring your previous work and share your strategy with your new partner.
- Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings.
Goal: Multiple representations + collaborative problem solving

◆ Showdown
Structure: Indy -> Group comparison -> Consensus
Setup: Groups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard. Rotate the Captain each round.
Directions:
- Each student solves the problem individually.
- When prompted, students Showdown (reveal answers at the same time).
- Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
- Group agrees on a correct final answer.
- Repeat.
Goal: Accountability + justification

◆ Think, Trade, Agree
Structure: Indy -> Group comparison -> Consensus
Setup: Groups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard.
Directions:
- Teacher presents problem.
- Each student solves the problem individually.
- When all students are done, exchange boards.
- Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
- Come to an agreement or ask teacher to help.
- Repeat exchange with someone different.
Goal: Accountability + justification

◆ Round Table
Structure: One step per student
Setup: Groups of 4. One whiteboard and one marker per group.
Directions:
- Groups of 4. One whiteboard and marker per group.
- Each student completes ONE step, then passes the board.
- Rotate until the problem is complete.
- Compare when the solution is shown.
- If everyone understands, celebrate.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility

◆ Mathematical Hot Seat
Structure: Verbal description only -> Blind solving -> Role rotation
Setup: Groups. One whiteboard and one marker per member.
Directions:
- One student per team sits facing away from the screen (the Hot Seat).
- Teacher projects a problem - only teammates can see it.
- Teammates describe the problem using words only - no gestures, no writing.
- Hot Seat student solves based solely on the verbal description.
- After solving, the student turns around and compares to the original problem.
- Rotate the Hot Seat for each round.
Goal: Mathematical vocabulary + precise communication

◆ Rally Coach II
Structure: Teammate lists steps other 3 write
Setup: Group of 4. Each person needs one whiteboard and one marker.
Directions:
- Pick Starter.
- Starter lists steps to solve; teammates write.
- Check answers with teacher.
- Rotate starter.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural accuracy

HANDOUT OPTIONS

◆ Stations
Structure: Timed rotations
Setup: Use the current Framework's station count/page contract. Students pick up a record sheet before beginning.
Directions:
- Get the record sheet.
- Choose a starting station within the posted group-size limit.
- Work until the teacher signals rotation.
- Rotate as directed; do not crowd one station.
Goal: Repetition + varied problem exposure

◆ Find Someone Who
Structure: Movement + peer interaction
Setup: Students need the printable worksheet/record sheet and a pencil.
Directions:
- Work alone first and attempt as many problems as possible.
- Find a partner and compare completed work.
- Work together on one unfinished or disagreed-on problem; both students record the reasoning.
- Find someone new and repeat.
- Return with the work completed and understood, not merely filled in.
Goal: Exposure to multiple explanations

OPTIONAL LINKED OPTIONS

◆ Desmos Activity
Structure: Individual or Partners -> Teacher-paced investigation
Setup: Individual or partner work, computer/iPad.
Directions:
- Open the teacher-selected activity using the code/link supplied by the teacher.
- Work through each screen thoughtfully.
- Pause for whole-class discussion when prompted.
Goal: Conceptual exploration + interactive sense-making
Rule: The Activities builder does NOT invent or claim a Desmos link that the teacher/Framework did not supply.

◆ Tarsia (Puzzle Activity)
Structure: Matching puzzle (self-checking)
Setup: Groups of 4. Copies of puzzle.
Directions:
- Students match expressions, answers, or steps.
- Pieces form a complete shape.
- If correct, the puzzle fits.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
Rule: When the current course owns Tarsia in Practice, the Activities hub may LINK to the existing Practice Tarsia/generator route but must not create a duplicate Tarsia bank/export.

A course Framework may intentionally hide a library option that does not fit that course. It may also approve additional structures, but additions must be explicit in the Framework; the builder does not freelance new ones.
