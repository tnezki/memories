------------------------------------------------
5A. INSTANTANEOUS RATE OF CHANGE
------------------------------------------------

Students are NOT formally differentiating yet.

Teach estimation from a graph by:
1. selecting the target point on a curve;
2. identifying its coordinates;
3. selecting a second point;
4. finding the secant slope;
5. moving the second point closer to the target;
6. comparing secant slopes;
7. estimating the slope of the tangent line / instantaneous rate of change.

Use simple quadratic and/or cubic curves with readable graph scales.

Emphasize:
- point coordinates matter;
- slope is change in y / change in x;
- the estimate should stabilize as the second point moves closer;
- instantaneous rate is a LOCAL slope idea.

------------------------------------------------
5B. AREA / LRAM
------------------------------------------------

Estimate area under a graph with LEFT RIEMANN SUM only.

Use approximately 5–10 rectangles.
Do not introduce a full taxonomy of Riemann sums.

Students should:
- read the interval;
- determine rectangle width;
- use left-endpoint heights;
- draw/see the rectangles;
- add rectangle areas;
- interpret the result as an estimate.

Use simple quadratic/cubic graphs with visually readable intervals.

8. INVESTIGATION — ABOUT 30 MINUTES
================================================================

Target length:

------------------------------------------------
8A. PART A — GUESS THE TANGENT LINE
------------------------------------------------

Use simple quadratic and cubic functions.

Students may use the Desmos calculator to guess/check.

Core workflow:
1. Graph the function.
2. Identify a target x-value.
3. Find the exact coordinates (x1, y1) of the point on the curve.
4. Estimate the tangent slope m visually / by nearby secants / guess-and-check.
5. Enter or write:

      y = m(x - x1) + y1

6. Adjust m until the line appears tangent at the chosen point.
7. Record the estimated slope and tangent-line equation.
8. Explain what visual evidence made the estimate reasonable.

IMPORTANT:
Emphasize TWO pieces:
- coordinates of the point;
- slope.

Then use point-slope form:
  y = m(x - x1) + y1

Do not formally teach derivative rules.

If no special Desmos activity/code exists:
- use ordinary Desmos calculator directions;
- do NOT fabricate a classroom code.

------------------------------------------------
8B. PART B — BUILD LRAM
------------------------------------------------

Use simple quadratic and cubic graphs.

Students should estimate area over manageable intervals.

For each task:
- provide a clean graph;
- specify interval and number of rectangles;
- keep number of rectangles around 5–10;
- students DRAW the left rectangles directly on the graph in Notability;
- record rectangle width;
- record/use left-endpoint heights;
- calculate total estimated area;
- briefly discuss whether the picture suggests under/over-estimation when meaningful.

The student document must contain graph surfaces large enough to draw on.

Keep the investigation exploratory and visual.
Do not turn it into 30 minutes of prose.

Provide a teacher guide with:
- intended flow;
- reasonable estimates;
- likely misconceptions;
- quick facilitation prompts;
- where to stop if time is short.
