CURRICULUM PHILOSOPHY AMENDMENT - PORTFOLIO / RECURRING EVIDENCE
================================================================
STATUS: CURRENT
SCOPE: universal authority boundaries for Portfolio status language, recurring evidence naming, intervention reporting, and canonical report templates.
REVISION: 2026-09-22 - LOCKED INTERVENTION REPORT + COURSE-AGNOSTIC SUPPORT ROUTING


This amendment clarifies the universal Philosophy without replacing its course-independent beliefs.


1. COURSE-SPECIFIC STATUS LANGUAGE
The universal Philosophy does NOT prescribe Not Yet / Approaching / Meeting / Exceeding, I/C/B/A/A+, or any other fixed proficiency vocabulary. The current Course Framework/policy owns exact Portfolio status labels and rollup rules. Legacy examples in Curriculum_Philosophy.txt are illustrative only and may not override current course policy.


2. COURSE-SPECIFIC RECURRING ARTIFACT NAMES
Terms such as Exit Ticket, Quick Check, Checkpoint, quiz, or short evidence pulse are course-specific implementation names. The current Course Framework owns the active name and mechanics. For Algebra 1, current policy uses Quick Check for the common short-evidence pulse and Checkpoint for the larger personalized evidence event. Legacy Philosophy references to Exit Tickets do not revive retired Algebra terminology or cadence.


3. BODY OF EVIDENCE
Assessment evidence is interpreted from the task actually asked and the student's response. Multiple target judgments may come from one task only when the task genuinely provides separately judgeable evidence for those targets. Multiple judgments from one source do not become independent opportunities merely because they map to different targets or appear in separate rows.


4. CANONICAL RECURRING TEMPLATE FIDELITY
When a registered recurring template/contract has been teacher-approved, that template owns both visual structure AND fixed recurring wording/section architecture. A PM fills the declared data slots; it may not casually rewrite headings, reflection prompts, feedback categories, table columns, card anatomy, or report organization from run to run. Intentional redesign requires teacher approval and promotion of the new template.


5. STUDENT FEEDBACK
Student-facing feedback should be concise, evidence-grounded, and stable enough that repeated reports are easy to understand. The Portfolio identifies what the evidence shows and what target to work on next; it does not invent a new remediation lesson or new practice questions inside the report when a reviewed course Bank supplies that practice.


5A. STATUS AND NEXT ACTION ARE DISTINCT
A proficiency status communicates the current body of evidence. A next-action label communicates what should happen next. Do not overload one status label to do both jobs. Two students may both be Developing while one needs practice before another check and the other already has one convincing demonstration and only needs another independent demonstration. Course policy/template may define a small stable next-action vocabulary so student reports remain consistent.


6. TEACHER SUMMARY
Teacher-facing Portfolio reporting is an intervention-planning tool, not a documentation dump. The report should answer, quickly: what needs a common class response, who gets the limited weekly teacher-led intervention seats first, and which students can be handled through short individualized pull-ins.


The canonical teacher report uses exactly these lanes:
- Whole Class
- Priority Day Groups
- Pull In Students


Detailed evidence remains available in private Portfolio data files. It does not need to be repeated as a long appendix in the normal teacher report.


6A. WHOLE CLASS
Use Whole Class only for a genuinely broad, shared instructional need supported by current evidence. Select the highest-leverage common target rather than listing every weak I Can. The page should identify the target, summarize the recurring evidence pattern, and give one concise teacher move. Keep the response short enough to fit naturally into normal class instruction.


6B. PRIORITY DAY CAPACITY AND RANKING
Priority Day represents limited weekly Crewtime capacity. The normal teacher report may show at most TWO Priority Day groups, with at most FIFTEEN students per group.


Priority ordering is based on current need, not alphabetical order and not exact bundle matching:
1. students with the greatest number of current assessed Mastery Goal grades of I come first;
2. ties are broken by number of current PRACTICE_AND_CHECK I Can gaps;
3. remaining ties use current evidence severity and then a stable identity order.


Only students who actually need practice/reteach belong in Priority Day. A student whose only next step is another independent demonstration or a first evidence check is not placed into a teacher-led intervention seat merely because the Mastery Goal grade is I.


The first fifteen eligible students become Priority Day Group 1. The next fifteen become Priority Day Group 2. A group does NOT require all students to share the same exact I Can bundle. Instead, the report summarizes the dominant overlapping needs and proposes the best common 20-minute Crewtime intervention, with short alternate practice only when needed for a small number of students.


6C. PULL IN STUDENTS
Pull In Students is student-centered. Include students with one or two current PRACTICE_AND_CHECK gaps that fit short individualized help while the teacher circulates among a few students.


The normal Pull In page is ONE landscape page. Use a compact multi-column grid when needed. State once, prominently, that everyone on the page needs `Practice + check soon`; do not repeat a separate PLAN label under every student. Each student entry shows the exact one or two I Can targets.


Priority Day and Pull In are different scheduling views and may overlap. A student can be high enough priority for the weekly teacher-led group and still remain in the Pull In queue for short targeted practice on another day.


Students who only need another independent demonstration are intentionally kept out of Pull In.


6D. TEACHER-CONTROLLED PRINT ERGONOMICS
A teacher-approved recurring assessment/report template may include non-print controls that adjust page allocation, problem/workspace height, and similar print geometry without changing the learning target or evidence demand. Efficient use of paper and toner is a legitimate design constraint: avoid decorative shading, unnecessary repeated headers, hidden names, and large unused areas. Layout changes must not alter question meaning, difficulty, evidence mapping, or accessibility.


6E. PRIVATE CROSS-DEVICE RESULTS ARCHIVE
A report-producing Portfolio run must preserve the exact returned results ZIP in the private Unit state area under `02 Portfolio Data / Results Archives`. Keep both a dated immutable archive copy and a predictable `Latest Portfolio Results.zip` current copy. This archive is private system state, not a human-facing shared-report folder. It exists so the same current output is available from school, home, or a later tool without relying on one computer's downloads.


6F. SUPPORT-STAFF ROUTING IS PRIVATE METADATA
Portfolio may maintain one private, course-agnostic support-routing source that links students to the staff responsible for supporting them. The canonical routing fields are `student_id | student_name | staff_name | staff_email | role | support_class | active`. A student may have multiple rows when multiple staff relationships apply. `support_class` is optional and may name any support/resource/study-skills class (for example Math Study Skills, English Support, Academic Strategies, Resource, or a future support course); leave it blank for a relationship such as a case manager that is not tied to a specific class. Keep this routing source separate from accommodation/modification rows and do not store diagnoses, disability narratives, or unrelated IEP details in it. Never infer an email address from a staff name. Student reports and report folders are not shared broadly to accomplish staff delivery; the email workflow may send the student's current report directly to active support staff with verified addresses.


6G. FAMILY LANGUAGE DISTINGUISHES UNCHECKED FROM UNSUCCESSFUL
`Not assessed` means the Mastery Goal has not been checked yet. `Needs first check` means the individual I Can has not been checked yet. Neither phrase means the student attempted the target and failed to show enough evidence. Once a target has been checked, use the applicable evidence/status/action language instead of describing it as unassessed.


6H. APPROVED RECURRING ARTIFACT WORKFLOW
When a course adopts a teacher selector/control page for a recurring assessment or Portfolio workflow, that page may become the normal build entry point. The teacher selects the operation and may add a small run-specific note; the build PM must preserve approved selections and the registered visual/template contract. Approved artifacts should remain easy to reopen, print, and audit from their canonical course location.


END - CURRICULUM PHILOSOPHY AMENDMENT
