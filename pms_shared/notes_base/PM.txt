================================================================================
NOTES PURPOSE / UNIVERSAL INSTRUCTIONAL PHILOSOPHY
================================================================================

Notes are a guided instructional bridge around the exact I-can structure and canonical
Bank anchors. They are not a script that must be completed top-to-bottom and they are not
a thin outline followed by a stack of problems.

Strong Notes combine:
  - exact Learning Target + I-can statements
  - canonical WTC
  - source-grounded, developed reading/explanation
  - purposeful annotation / Reading Apprenticeship
  - actual mathematical/scientific representations
  - an immediate Stop and Discuss after EVERY substantial reading
  - additional one-question discussion checkpoints later in an I-can cluster when useful
  - useful Math Notes / Model Notes as durable reference, with abundance allowed
  - canonical Examples/YTIs placed near the instruction that supports them
  - vocabulary/notation when useful, developed naturally in context
  - Summary
  - section-specific Common Mistakes / misconceptions

Do not put all exposition first and all canonical application items last.
Do not force one reading cycle per Bank pair.
Do not force a Stop and Discuss after every YTI; the required discussion follows substantial reading.
Do not force a Math/Model Notes block before every pair, but do not underbuild useful reference notes merely to
make the document shorter.
Do not create filler reading to separate pairs.

The Framework may make the section structure more compartmentalized by exact I-can,
more source-driven, more diagram-driven, more theorem-driven, or otherwise course-
specific. Follow the Framework's Notes profile.

================================================================================
READING / EXPLANATION / REPRESENTATION
================================================================================

Source provenance guides the builder, not student-facing prose. Do not write student
lines such as "The textbook says," "The Assessment Plan says," "The Bank uses," or
internal build commentary unless attribution itself is instructionally necessary.

Paraphrase protected source text. Do not copy long passages verbatim.

Explain as appropriate:
  - what students are looking at
  - which quantities/variables/features matter
  - what changes and what stays the same
  - why a procedure/model works
  - how representations connect
  - how to interpret a result
  - assumptions, conditions, or limitations

When representation reasoning matters, SHOW the actual graph/table/equation/model rather
than merely talking about one.

Reading density/tone is course-specific and comes from the Framework. The universal guardrail is that a
substantial reading must actually develop the idea: do not reduce it to one thin paragraph or a disguised
procedure list merely to get to the next question.

When the section has a Vocabulary / Key Notation preview, bold a target vocabulary/notation term at its FIRST
meaningful introduction or definition in the instructional prose. Do not bold every recurrence.

================================================================================
STOP AND DISCUSS - REQUIRED AFTER SUBSTANTIAL READING
================================================================================

Every substantial instructional reading is followed IMMEDIATELY by a Stop and Discuss before the next major
application block. The discussion belongs to the reading/representation sense-making cycle, not automatically
to every Example/YTI.

Default main Stop and Discuss after a substantial reading:
  - 2 to 3 questions total
  - 1 or 2 questions answerable directly from the reading / vocabulary / shown representation
  - the remaining question(s) require inference, connection, comparison, explanation, prediction, or another
    conceptual move beyond copying a sentence

Later inside the SAME I-can cluster, add an additional Stop and Discuss only when a meaningful checkpoint
exists. That later checkpoint should normally be ONE focused question highlighting an important connection,
misconception, representation feature, or inference.

Questions must be specific to the actual reading/representations. Do not reuse a stock comprehension shell.

================================================================================
MATH NOTES / MODEL NOTES - USEFUL REFERENCE ABUNDANCE
================================================================================

Math Notes / Model Notes are durable reference artifacts. More can be better when each block is genuinely
useful; the teacher may skip. Do not limit reference notes merely because not every block will be used live.

Prefer concrete artifacts over meta-language:
  - show the equation, table, graph, diagram, model, notation, worked relationship, or comparison
  - explain what each feature means and how the forms connect
  - include a compact worked/reference example when it improves rereading
  - use multiple blocks when they serve distinct high-value ideas

The Framework owns the course-specific representation system and decides what a strong Math/Model Notes block
looks like.

THREE-COLUMN VOCABULARY SORT MARKUP — HARD CONTRACT WHEN THIS SORT IS USED
The current Notes CSS already owns the usable writing-space geometry. The renderer must emit the classes
on the elements the stylesheet is designed to target. Do not compensate with inline height/style patches.

Use this structure:

  <section class="vocab-preview-section">

    <p class="vocab-list">...</p>
    <div class="vocab-sort-task">
      <p class="vocab-sort-directions">...</p>

        <thead>...</thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr class="vocab-sort-space"><td></td><td></td><td></td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

Element/class placement is exact:
  - `vocab-sort-table` belongs on the TABLE, not `vocab-sort`.
  - `vocab-sort-space` belongs on the blank BODY ROW (`tr`), not on each `td`.
  - sort directions use `vocab-sort-directions` and sit inside `vocab-sort-task`.
  - do not shrink/remove the blank sort row; the deployed Notes CSS controls its writing height.

A build that asks students to sort vocabulary but emits a shallow one-line row because these classes are
missing or attached to the wrong elements is a renderer/layout defect and must be repaired before delivery.

Do not pad a vocabulary box with familiar words merely to fill a template. Do not bold every later occurrence.

TEACHER-GUIDE DISCOURSE MOVES — REQUIRED
----------------------------------------
Teacher Notes also carry one compact live-teaching discourse reminder for EACH visible I-can
instructional cluster.

For each I-can:
- add ONE teacher-only `Discourse Moves` block;
- place it in the nearest corresponding teacher-support/workspace flow after the most useful
  Math Notes / diagram / representation for that I-can when possible; teacher workspaces are
  natural-height and may expand freely;
- if no Math Notes/diagram block exists, place it after the first substantial instructional
  representation/reading for that I-can;
- use existing red/bold teacher-support styling; do NOT create new CSS/classes.

Format:

  Discourse Moves
  Teacher Moves
  - 2–4 brief moves tailored to this exact I-can
  Student Moves
  - 2–4 brief moves tailored to this exact I-can

Teacher move families: wait, invite multiple ideas, revoice, ask another student to restate,
probe for evidence, compare/connect representations, engage with another student's reasoning.

Student move families: explain, clarify, restate, compare, support with evidence, build on,
challenge.

The bullet wording should reference the actual mathematics when useful. For example,
"What in the graph supports that claim?" is better than a generic "Explain your thinking."

Use the exact I-can, representation, Stop-and-Discuss questions, and likely misconceptions to
choose the moves. Repeated generic boilerplate across I-cans is a failure.

The STUDENT Notes HTML receives no Discourse Moves block.
