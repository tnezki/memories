# Question Starter Stem Library v1

Purpose: provide flexible openings for strong math/science questions without becoming a canned-question generator.
These are STARTERS, not scripts. Rewrite naturally. Fill every placeholder with content-specific language.
Never repeat a distinctive starter across unrelated families just because it worked once.

## Hard anti-pattern rule

A starter is invalid if the completed question still has no specific evidence target.
Avoid stand-alone prompts such as:
- Explain your reasoning.
- Analyze the graph.
- Compare the results.
- Describe the relationship.
- What do you notice?
- What happens?
- Which method is better?
- Discuss.
- Justify.

Those phrases can be used only when the prompt also names the evidence, feature, decision, or conclusion students must address.

## 1. Direct procedural / fluency

1. "Find [quantity] using [given information]. Show the step where you [critical operation]."
2. "Solve [equation/model] for [target variable], then check the result by [specific check]."
3. "Evaluate [expression] at [input] and state [unit/meaning] of the result."
4. "Rewrite [expression] in [target form] so that [specific feature] is visible."
5. "Complete [procedure] and identify the first step at which [common error] could occur."
6. "Use [formula/rule] to determine [quantity]; keep units attached through each step."

## 2. Strategy selection

7. "Which method is most efficient for [specific problem], and what structural feature makes that method a good choice?"
8. "Before solving, classify this as a [method family] problem and name the feature that determines your choice."
9. "Choose between [method A] and [method B] for this instance. Solve using your choice and explain the deciding feature."
10. "Which theorem/rule applies here? Verify the condition that allows you to use it before applying it."
11. "What should be done first: [A], [B], or [C]? Defend the choice using the structure of [object]."
12. "Identify the operation that unlocks this problem, then carry it out."

## 3. Representation transfer

13. "Use the [table] to construct a [graph/equation] that preserves [specific feature]."
14. "The [graph] and [equation] describe the same relationship. Show where [feature] appears in each representation."
15. "Translate the statement '[verbal relationship]' into an equation, but do not solve it."
16. "Write a context that could be modeled by [equation/graph], and explain what [parameter] means in your context."
17. "Complete the missing values in the table using the rule represented by [equation/graph]."
18. "Sketch a graph consistent with [listed constraints], then label where each constraint appears."
19. "From the graph alone, determine [behavior] and cite the feature that supports your conclusion."
20. "Convert [representation A] to [representation B], then identify one feature that is easier to see in the new form."

## 4. Graphical reasoning

21. "On which interval is [quantity] [increasing/decreasing/positive/negative]? Use [slope/sign/shape] as evidence."
22. "At [location], what does the graph imply about [rate/acceleration/derivative]? Cite the local graphical feature."
23. "Identify a point where [condition] holds and explain how the graph shows it."
24. "Sketch one possible graph satisfying [conditions]. Verify each condition after sketching."
25. "The graph has [corner/jump/asymptote]. Determine whether [quantity] exists there and explain why."
26. "Compare [two graphical features] and determine which interval shows the greater [rate/change]."
27. "Use the area/sign/slope shown on the graph to determine [linked quantity]."
28. "Tell a plausible story that matches every major feature of this graph, including [specific feature]."

## 5. Table / data reasoning

29. "Use the table to determine whether the relationship has [constant difference/constant ratio/other pattern]. Show the evidence."
30. "Estimate [rate] at [target] using the most appropriate nearby table values and include units."
31. "Which entries in the table are needed to answer [question], and which are not? Explain the selection."
32. "Complete the missing cells using the governing relationship, not simple pattern continuation."
33. "Which model family best fits the data? Use [difference/ratio/residual/shape] evidence."
34. "Identify the outlier and explain how it changes [model/prediction/summary]."
35. "Use two pieces of data to support or reject the claim that [specific claim]."
36. "Predict [future/intermediate value] from the model and judge how reliable that prediction is."

## 6. Error analysis / misconception

37. "A student writes [worked step]. Identify the first incorrect step, name the misconception, and repair the work from that point."
38. "The final answer is correct, but one justification is invalid. Identify the invalid reasoning and replace it."
39. "A student claims [plausible claim]. Decide whether it is always true, sometimes true, or false, and support the decision with [specific evidence]."
40. "Which of these two solutions is mathematically valid? Focus on [specific condition/step], not just the final answer."
41. "The diagram/model includes one impossible feature. Identify it and revise the model so it is physically/mathematically consistent."
42. "A calculator returns [result]. Use [units/sign/magnitude/domain] to decide whether the result is reasonable."

## 7. Comparison / method meaning

43. "Both methods reach the same result. What does [method A] reveal that [method B] does not?"
44. "Compare the two models on [specific criterion: starting value/rate/domain/error]. Which better fits the stated goal?"
45. "How are [representation A] and [representation B] expressing the same [concept]? Name the matching features."
46. "Which result is more sensitive to a change in [parameter], and what evidence shows this?"
47. "Compare [case 1] and [case 2]. Identify the one condition that changes the conclusion."
48. "Two valid strategies are shown. Under what conditions would each be preferable?"

## 8. Theorem / condition / existence

49. "Can [theorem] be applied on [interval/case]? Check each required condition before stating the conclusion."
50. "Which hypothesis fails, and what conclusion can no longer be guaranteed because of that failure?"
51. "State whether [quantity] exists. If it does, determine it; if not, identify the condition that fails."
52. "Give an example that satisfies [some conditions] but not [target conclusion], or explain why no such example can exist."
53. "Construct a counterexample to the claim [claim]. Your example must satisfy [stated premise]."
54. "Show why [claim] holds for every allowed value, not just the examples shown."

## 9. Modeling / context

55. "Define variables for [situation], build a model, and state the domain that makes sense in context."
56. "Two processes are described. Build a model for each and determine when their outputs are equal."
57. "Which model is appropriate for [data/context]? Name the structural evidence before fitting the model."
58. "Use the model to predict [quantity], then interpret the result in the original context with units."
59. "The model gives [mathematically valid result]. Decide whether it is physically/contextually valid and explain the restriction."
60. "What information is missing before this situation can be modeled? Explain why that information is necessary."
61. "Which given details are irrelevant to the requested quantity? Solve using only the needed information."
62. "Create a context that matches [equation/table/graph] and explain the meaning of each parameter."

## 10. Estimation / reasonableness

63. "Before calculating, estimate [quantity] using [rounding/bounds/reference value]. Then compare to the exact result."
64. "Without computing the exact value, determine whether [approximation] is an overestimate or underestimate and justify using [monotonicity/concavity/structure]."
65. "A student obtained [value]. Use units, sign, and order of magnitude to judge whether it is plausible."
66. "Give a lower and upper bound for [quantity] using [available condition/data]."
67. "Which answer choice can be eliminated by estimation alone? State the estimate that eliminates it."
68. "Predict the direction of change before calculating; then use the calculation to confirm or revise the prediction."

## 11. Science mechanism / CER

69. "State a claim about [phenomenon], cite the specific observation/data that supports it, and name the principle connecting the evidence to the claim."
70. "Explain why [phenomenon] occurs by tracing the causal mechanism from [initial condition] to [observed result]."
71. "Use the free-body diagram to determine [net force/acceleration] and identify which force relationship drives the result."
72. "Draw a free-body diagram for [specified object only], then use it to determine [conclusion]."
73. "Predict how changing [independent variable] affects [dependent variable], holding [controls] constant, and explain the mechanism."
74. "Design a test of [relationship]. Identify the manipulated, measured, and controlled variables and explain one important confound."
75. "Use dimensional analysis to decide whether [proposed equation] could represent [physical quantity]."
76. "Compare the prediction from [model/principle] with the observed data and identify one reason for any discrepancy."

## 12. Inverse / reverse reasoning

77. "The output is [value]. Determine the input(s) that could have produced it and apply any domain/context restrictions."
78. "Given [rate/derivative/velocity] information, reconstruct one possible [original function/position] consistent with it."
79. "Work backward from [final state] to determine the missing initial condition."
80. "The graph shows the derivative/rate. Sketch one possible original function and label the features forced by the given graph."
81. "Given the target answer [value/equation], create a valid problem/context that would produce it."
82. "A final measurement is known. Determine which earlier quantity must have had what value for the model to produce that result."

## 13. Open construction / constraints

83. "Create a [graph/model/problem] satisfying all of these constraints: [constraints]. Verify each one."
84. "Find more than one valid object satisfying [conditions], then identify what all valid examples have in common."
85. "Arrange [fixed values] to maximize/minimize [expression]. Explain the structural reason your arrangement is optimal."
86. "Write a problem of type [target structure] whose solution has [constraint], then solve your own problem."
87. "Describe [object] precisely enough that another student could reproduce it without seeing it."
88. "Construct two examples that meet [condition] but differ in [feature], then explain why both are valid."

## 14. Warm-Up / quick diagnostic

89. "Which answer best represents the error made when [specific misconception]?"
90. "Without fully solving, which strategy should be used first for [problem], and why?"
91. "Which representation matches [given relationship]? Focus on [specific feature]."
92. "Estimate first: which interval/value should the exact answer fall in?"
93. "Which one does not belong? Choose one and justify with a specific property. More than one answer may be defensible."
94. "Find the pair of values satisfying both [condition 1] and [condition 2]."
95. "What additional information would make this problem solvable?"
96. "Which statement is always true for [defined object]? Use the definition, not appearance."

## 15. Exit Ticket / evidence check

97. "Use [specific representation] to determine [target] and give one piece of evidence that distinguishes your answer from [common misconception]."
98. "Solve [target task], then state what your result means about [specific concept]."
99. "Decide whether [claim] is supported by today's model/data and cite the decisive evidence."
100. "Identify the condition that must be true for [method/theorem] to apply, then decide whether it holds here."
101. "Complete one example of [skill] and one reverse-direction example that tests the same relationship."
102. "What is the smallest change to [given object] that would make [target property] true? Explain why that change works."

## 16. AP-style connected multi-part

103. "Using the shared [table/graph/function/context], (a) determine [local quantity], (b) interpret it, and (c) use it to estimate/predict [connected quantity]."
104. "For the same function, (a) verify [condition], (b) apply [theorem], and (c) interpret the conclusion in context."
105. "From the graph of [f/rate], (a) evaluate [accumulated value], (b) determine [derivative relationship], and (c) analyze [concavity/existence] at [point]."
106. "A parameterized family is given. First compute [result] in terms of the parameter, then show whether [specific feature] depends on that parameter."
107. "Use the table to estimate [rate], then use the estimate consistently in a later part. Later scoring should reward correct follow-through."
108. "Determine whether [requested value] exists; if it does not, identify the exact condition/feature causing nonexistence before continuing to the next connected part."

## 17. Coherent cross-concept synthesis

109. "Use [skill A] to produce [intermediate result]; then use that result as the input to [skill B] to answer [larger question]."
110. "Represent [same object] in [form A] and [form B], then use the connection to determine [target]."
111. "Check [condition] first; only if it holds, apply [rule/theorem] to determine [target]."
112. "Use [data/observation] to establish [claim], then use [principle] to explain why the claim follows."
113. "Compare [two cases] to isolate which variable/condition causes the different outcomes."
114. "Retrieve [prior skill] because it is required to complete [current skill]; state where the prior result enters the current work."

## Bad -> better examples

BAD: "Solve the equation. Explain your reasoning."
BETTER: "Solve the equation and identify the inverse operation used to undo the coefficient."

BAD: "Analyze the graph."
BETTER: "Identify where the function changes from increasing to decreasing and use that change to locate a local maximum."

BAD: "Compare the results."
BETTER: "Compare the two rates of change and determine which plan becomes more expensive after the break-even point."

BAD: "What do you notice about this table?"
BETTER (graded): "Determine whether the table shows a constant difference or constant ratio and use the pattern to classify the model."
BETTER (lesson opener): "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" is fine when open noticing is the actual architecture.

BAD: "Find the slope, then factor x^2 + 5x + 6."
BETTER: Split into two questions unless the factoring result is genuinely needed for the slope task.

BAD: "Find acceleration, then define momentum."
BETTER: "Find acceleration from the velocity data, then use the object's mass to determine the net force."

BAD: "Differentiate f, then evaluate an unrelated integral."
BETTER: "Differentiate f, identify its critical points, and use the sign of f' to classify the extrema."

BAD: "Student A says the answer is 6. Student B says 8. Who is correct?"
BETTER: Use Student A/B only when comparing real reasoning is the target; otherwise ask the mathematical question directly.

BAD: "A correct analysis gives 12 m/s. Which choice is correct?"
BETTER: Remove the supplied conclusion and require the student to determine the speed from the data/model.

BAD: "A graph approaches 4 from both sides. What is the limit?"
BETTER when graphical reasoning is intended: provide the graph and ask the student to determine the limit from the displayed behavior.

BAD: "Use at least two data points to support your conclusion." repeated across dozens of unrelated items.
BETTER: state the evidence actually needed for that specific item; sometimes one decisive feature is enough, sometimes several are required.
