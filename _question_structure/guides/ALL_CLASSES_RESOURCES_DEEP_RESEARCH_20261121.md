# Deep resource-inspection pass — November 21, 2026

**Status:** Research only. No changes to current bank, Builder, lessons or teacher-review state.

**Scope:** 56 observed task structures / workflows across 22 source groups (53 newly evidenced in this pass; 3 cited from the prior POD pass). The source descriptions below are paraphrased from inspected pages, not reproduced questions.

## What changed compared with the quick map

- The quick map identified resource capability by broad source group. This pass examined particular pages or legacy Word lab instructions, recorded what students actually *do*, and tied each observation to a transfer use.
- The highest-value short extensions are error analysis, representation transfer, model checking, reverse questions, counterexamples and conditions/constraints; higher numbers or a generic “explain” prompt do not count.
- Separate investigations/labs from warm-ups: rich labs should not be compressed into a 3-minute question, but their **reasoning pattern** can sometimes inspire a brief extension.
- The previously reviewed POD set remains its own catalog and is not re-counted as new families.

## Especially useful discoveries

**Big Ideas Algebra 1 (chapter 4):** actual explicit error-analysis items challenge point substitution, perpendicular slopes and the meaning of regression outputs (PDF pp. 15, 27, 42). These are direct models of a new *student action*, not added complexity.

**Glencoe Enrichment:** growing toothpick structures, closure/counterexamples, exponent-variation meaning, packing optimization and critique of a counting claim (PDF pp. 1, 4, 25, 66, 79). It contains a richer range of structures than the practice workbook alone.

**EMPower Algebraic Thinking:** a set of tables changes from straightforward patterns to another rule type (PDF p. 18); elsewhere students choose arithmetic operations under constraints (p. 35), and interpret multiple real-world rate graphs (p. 60). These can be adapted as 1–2-minute transfer kernels.

**Exeter:** long integrated sequences explicitly interleave diagramming, data interpretation, rates and units, inverse model selection, transformations and checks of general claims (Math 1 pp. 16, 26, 40, 58, 83; Math 3 pp. 7, 13, 39, 58, 92). The full problems are often too long for warm-ups; adapt the *thinking move*, not the entire prompt.

**Vernier Real World Math:** complete instructions reveal genuine lab cycles: measure → represent → model → check against observation. Examples include velocity area vs distance, two-walker line intersection and exponential bounce decay. These are better suited to future STEM/Physics and Algebra modeling lessons than a routine practice bank.

**101 Mathematical Projects:** sampled chapters cover ergonomics, sports data, board-path enumeration, household energy costs, stopping distances and rolling geometry (PDF pp. 22, 35, 46, 67, 88, 110). This is an especially broad resource for student-led project choices.

## Reality checks and gaps

- **Actual page inspection:** New page-position evidence is provided in the JSON under `verification`. Scanned sources were visually read at selected pages; PDF text was extracted at selected pages; the five legacy Vernier lab documents were opened and checked.
- **Not exhaustive:** Beyond these positions, no conclusion is claimed about every page. Publisher books, files in compressed ZIPs, and most legacy `.bnk` question banks are not decoded here.
- **Source quality:** Some data contexts, instrument names and examples are dated; preserve the underlying mathematical structure, not outdated technology, statistics or cultural assumptions.
- **New family count:** Zero canonical generator families were created or approved. These are candidate structures, not bank-ready questions.
- **Copyright:** Publishers and third-party source materials remain references; do not reproduce their original text or figures in redistributed worksheets.
- **Source authority:** Current course Frameworks / Assessment Plans and canonical Question Structures still govern alignment. CPM Review & Preview remains a separate textbook-original workflow.

## Practical uses for the next step

**Warm-up bank:** Preserve Q1/Q2 fluency choices. For each Q3/Q4 first ask: what skill and what evidence did this base problem elicit? For optional Q5/Q6 choose a thinking move that makes students use *that same skill* under a representation change, method critique, missing-information test, boundary condition or novel case. Select from at most four distinct extension families per lesson as previously agreed.

**Future courses:** Use the project/lab rows as leads for actual Algebra, Geometry, Precalculus, Physics and STEM activities. Before building a lesson, revisit the original file and check equipment and accessible diagram needs.
